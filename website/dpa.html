Data Processing Agreement

Effective as of 16 November 2026 · Download PDF

Sessionly Data Processing Agreement. The current version is published at https://sessionly.dev/dpa.

In short

  • When your organization uploads its engineers' sessions to the hosted Sessionly service, your organization is the controller and I, Kristiyan Ivanov, am its processor. This agreement sets out the terms Article 28 GDPR requires for that.
  • It forms part of the Terms of Service and applies automatically when you use the Hosted Service. No signature is needed; if your records need a countersigned copy, email me and I will return one.
  • The Hosted Service is open at app.sessionly.dev and free during early access. It runs the published code, at DigitalOcean in Frankfurt, Germany (Annex III).
  • Full prompts, file contents, command output and source code are never uploaded. What is stored is a record of each session, by default without any text, with the security measures in Annex II.
  • Sub-processors are listed in Annex III. I will tell you 30 days before adding one, and you may object.
  • I will tell you about a personal data breach affecting your data without undue delay and in any case within 48 hours of becoming aware of it.
  • Local use and a self-hosted server are not covered: I process nothing for you there.

1. Definitions

  • “Data Protection Law” means Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (the “GDPR”), the GDPR as it forms part of the law of the United Kingdom (the “UK GDPR”) and the UK Data Protection Act 2018, the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection, and any national law implementing or supplementing them, in each case as they apply to the processing under this DPA.
  • “Customer Data” means personal data that you, your machines, workflows or team members upload to the Hosted Service, as described in Annex I. It includes the session records your engineers' machines upload, the pull request reports the Sessionly GitHub Action sends, and the names, email addresses and roles you attach to access tokens.
  • “Hosted Service” has the meaning given in the Terms: the hosted Sessionly service, including its dashboard and API, and the sessionly command-line program and GitHub Action when they talk to it.
  • “Sub-processor” means a third party I engage to process Customer Data on my behalf.
  • “Personal data,” “controller,” “processor,” “data subject,” “processing,” “personal data breach” and “supervisory authority” have the meaning given in the GDPR.

2. Scope and roles

2.1 You are the controller, I am the processor

For Customer Data, you are the controller (or a processor acting for your own customers, in which case you warrant that your instructions to me are covered by their instructions to you) and I am your processor. I process Customer Data only to provide the Hosted Service to you under the Terms and this DPA.

2.2 What this DPA does not cover

  • Data for which I am the controller. The email address and name you use to open an account or order a License, billing data and support correspondence are processed by me as controller, as described in the Privacy Policy. Where the same piece of data serves both purposes (for example a team member's email address, which I need to recognise their token and which you entered to give them access to your org), this DPA governs my processing of it on your instructions and the Privacy Policy governs my own.
  • What stays on your engineers' machines. The sessionly program keeps its raw capture, its ledger and its configuration on the machine it runs on. That data, which includes full prompts and file contents, never reaches me, and I do not process it.
  • Coding agents and GitHub. The makers of the coding agents your engineers use, and GitHub, process your data under your agreements with them. They are not my Sub-processors, and I do not control what they do. The GitHub Action runs in your GitHub account, on your instruction.
  • Self-hosted installs. If you run the Sessionly server on your own infrastructure, I do not process any data for you and this DPA does not apply. A Business or Enterprise License is verified offline and sends nothing to me.

3. Details of the processing

The subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing, the categories of data subjects and the categories of personal data are set out in Annex I. In outline: the sessionly program on each engineer's machine builds a record of each coding agent session, applies the content level chosen on that machine, and uploads the record; the GitHub Action reports pull requests as hashes of the lines they add; the Hosted Service stores both, matches them, and shows totals and individual sessions to the people in your org whose tokens allow it. Full prompts, file contents, command output and source code are never uploaded.

A personal data breach means a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, Customer Data. Unsuccessful attempts (such as requests with an unknown token or port scans) are not breaches and will not be notified.

9. Deletion and return

9.1 During the term

  • Deletion on request. I will delete particular sessions, a person's sessions or a repository's reports when an admin of your org asks me to by email.
  • Export. At any time an admin can read every session and report through the API as JSON.

9.2 At the end

When the Terms end for your org, or earlier if you ask me in writing to delete the org, I will delete all Customer Data within a reasonable time, normally within 30 days, including sessions, pull request reports and tokens, and I will confirm the deletion by email on request. Two exceptions: data I must keep under EU or Member State law is kept only as long as that law requires and remains protected by this DPA; and copies in database backups persist until the backups rotate, seven days later, during which they are used only to recover the service. Export what you need before closing your account, because after deletion I cannot return it. The data on your engineers' machines is not affected.

Annex I: Details of processing

Parties

  • Controller (data exporter): the Customer, the organization holding the org on the Hosted Service, acting through its admins.
  • Processor (data importer): Kristiyan Ivanov, self-employed individual registered in Bulgaria (BULSTAT 180490275), ul. Vasil Petleshkov 31-33, apt. 17, 1510 Sofia, Bulgaria, kristiyan@sessionly.dev.

Subject matter

Operation of the Hosted Service for the Customer: a server that receives a record of each AI coding agent session from the Customer's engineers' machines and reports of the Customer's pull requests, and shows what the sessions cost, what they changed and how much of it survived, with the dashboard and API around it.

Duration

For as long as the Customer holds an org on the Hosted Service, plus the deletion period in Section 9.2 of the DPA.

Nature of the processing

  • Receiving the session records the Customer's machines upload and the pull request reports its workflows send; checking each against the published schema; storing it, filed under the owner of the token that sent it.
  • Matching the hashed lines of a pull request against the hashed lines of sessions by the people who committed to it; summing sessions per person, repository, model, agent and day; showing sessions, totals and pull requests in the dashboard and the API to the people whose tokens allow it.
  • Hosting, backing up and deleting the above.

Purpose

To show the Customer what its coding agent sessions were, what they cost and how much of what agents wrote survived, per person, repository, model and agent, and otherwise to provide the Hosted Service as described in the Terms. No other purpose.

Categories of data subjects

  • The Customer's engineers: employees, contractors and others whose machines upload sessions to the Customer's org.
  • People with access: the admins, members and others the Customer issues tokens to.
  • Pull request authors, reviewers and commenters: people who open, review or comment on pull requests in repositories where the Customer runs the GitHub Action, whether or not they use Sessionly.
  • Anyone mentioned in a preview, if the Customer uploads previews: a prompt or command can name or describe any person.

Categories of personal data

  • Never uploaded: full prompts, the agent's replies, file contents, the output of commands, source code, and absolute paths under a home directory. They stay on the engineer's machine.
  • Session records (always): a random machine identifier; the agent and session identifier; the git user name and email address and the repository's remote address, as configured on the machine, or, for a session that ran in the cloud, the email address the platform gives for the person who started it; start and end times, active time and the timing of each turn; counts of prompts, tool calls, subagents and permission requests; for each tool call its tool, status, duration and output size; the files read or changed, as paths relative to the repository or home directory, with lines added and removed; models, token counts and cost; the rewrite measurement per file. Together these show when and on what a person worked with an agent.
  • Previews (only if switched on): the first 200 characters of each prompt; a one-line summary of each tool call (shell command, search pattern or URL, cut to 200 characters); the first 200 characters of an error; the session's title; SHA-256 hashes of prompts and tool inputs. Free text, which may contain any personal data the engineer typed.
  • Line hashes (only if switched on): per changed file and model, the first 8 bytes of the SHA-256 of each line the agent added.
  • Pull request reports: repository name, pull request number, title and address; the author's GitHub user name; state, branch names and dates; counts of commits, reviews, requests for changes, review comments by people and by bots, and review rounds; per changed file, hashes of the added lines; for each review and comment, its author's GitHub user name and whether that is a bot, when it was made, the file and line it is on, a review's outcome and its address on GitHub, and never its text. The email addresses on the pull request's commits are received with each report, used to select the sessions to compare with, and not stored.
  • Tokens: the email address (stored lower-case), optional name and role each token belongs to; the token's SHA-256 hash; when it was created, last used and revoked.

Special categories of data

None intended. The Hosted Service is not designed for special categories of personal data or data about criminal convictions; the Customer must not upload such data (Section 11 of the DPA).

Frequency, retention and location

  • Frequency: continuous: a connected machine uploads new and changed sessions when a turn ends (at most every five minutes) and when a session ends, and the GitHub Action reports when a pull request is opened, pushed to, reviewed or closed.
  • Retention: sessions, pull request reports and tokens while the org exists, unless the Order states a shorter period or the Customer asks for deletion earlier.
  • Location: Frankfurt, Germany (DigitalOcean's FRA1 region), as stated in Annex III.

Annex II: Technical and organisational measures

These are the measures in place as of the effective date, written from the code, which is public at github.com/KIvanow/sessionly. The security page describes them in more detail. The measures that depend on where the Hosted Service runs are under “Hosting” below. Sessionly has no SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certification.

Data minimisation, before anything is uploaded

  • Full prompts, file contents and command output stay in the raw capture on the engineer's machine, in files and directories readable only by that user (modes 0600 and 0700). The session record holds metadata and 200-character previews, not content.
  • Before upload, file paths are made relative to the repository or to the home directory, and the content level is applied. The default level, metadata, removes all text from prompts, commands, search patterns, URLs, errors and titles, and all content hashes. Previews and line hashes are each a separate, deliberate switch on the machine.
  • sessionly export prints the exact payload for any session, so an engineer can see what would be sent before connecting to a server. The payload's JSON Schema is published.
  • Pull requests and sessions are compared as hashes of lines. No source code is sent to the Hosted Service by the program or by the GitHub Action.

Hosting

  • The Hosted Service runs on one virtual machine at DigitalOcean in Frankfurt, Germany, with Customer Data in a DigitalOcean managed PostgreSQL database in the same region. Customer Data is stored nowhere else.
  • It is reached over HTTPS only, with a certificate from Let's Encrypt; plain HTTP is redirected and Strict-Transport-Security is sent. From the internet the machine answers on ports 80 and 443 only.
  • The database accepts connections from that machine alone. The server connects to it over the provider's private network, with TLS, and verifies the database's certificate against the provider's certificate authority.
  • The database is encrypted at rest by DigitalOcean. It is backed up daily, with what is needed to restore it to any moment of the previous seven days; backups older than that are gone. There are no other copies of Customer Data.
  • The machine is administered over SSH with a key, from named addresses; passwords are not accepted, and security updates of its system are applied automatically.

Separation of Customers

  • Every row of Customer Data belongs to one org, and every query names the org of the token that asked. Underneath, each request runs in the database as a role that cannot log in and that row-level security confines to that org: it can neither read nor write another org's rows.

Encryption in transit

  • The sessionly program refuses to connect to a server over plain HTTP unless the server is on the same machine, so uploads to the Hosted Service always use HTTPS.

Access control and authentication

  • Access is by bearer token. Tokens are generated from 24 random bytes, shown once when created, and stored only as SHA-256 hashes. They can be revoked at any time; when each was last used is recorded. There are no passwords.
  • People sign in to the dashboard with a Google or a GitHub account. Only which account it is and its verified email addresses are asked of the provider. The account that first signs in to an org under an address is from then on the only one accepted as that person there; an admin of the org, or the Processor, can let the person in with another. A sign-in lasts seven days.
  • Each token has one role. A member reads only the sessions filed under their own email address. An admin reads the whole org and manages tokens. A ci token can report pull requests and check that it is accepted, and is refused everything else.
  • An upload is filed under the owner of the token that sent it, whatever name the record carries, and a session cannot be overwritten by a different person.
  • On the engineer's machine the token is kept in a configuration file readable only by that user and is never printed.
  • Operationally, one person, the Processor, administers the Hosted Service. No employees or contractors have access to production systems or Customer Data.

Integrity

  • Every upload is validated against the published schema and every pull request report is checked field by field before it is stored; one that does not match is refused. Uploads are limited to 32 MB.
  • The capture hook on the engineer's machine only observes: it cannot block or change an agent's session, and events that would change the agent's behaviour when hooked are not registered.

Platform

  • The server does not write a log of requests, and the program and the server send no telemetry to me or to anyone else. License files are verified offline.
  • The dashboard counts which of its pages are opened, in PostHog's EU cloud. A count is the page's name, whether the server is the Hosted Service or a self-hosted one, and an id made up for that one load of the page. It contains no Customer Data: no org, person, session, token or anything a page shows. It is sent by the dashboard's own code, with no analytics library, as a request to which the browser adds no cookie and no header naming the site it comes from; nothing is stored in the browser for it; and a browser that sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control is not counted.
  • The website is static and loads one script, Vercel Web Analytics, from the site itself. It counts visits without cookies and without keeping IP addresses. The website is served with a Content Security Policy of default-src 'none' with only its own script, stylesheet, fonts and images and connections back to itself allowed, plus X-Content-Type-Options, X-Frame-Options and Referrer-Policy headers. No Customer Data is on the website.

Incident handling and transparency

  • Personal data breaches affecting Customer Data are notified to the Customer without undue delay and in any case within 48 hours of my becoming aware of them (Section 8 of the DPA).
  • Security problems can be reported to kristiyan@sessionly.dev, which I read myself. The whole codebase, including the capture program, the server and the GitHub Action, is public, so the measures above can be verified by reading it.
